**Boosting a post** is the quick way to pay Meta to show something you already posted on Facebook or Instagram to more people: you tap Boost, pick a simple goal, an audience, a budget and a number of days, and it runs. **Meta Ads Manager** is the full advertising tool behind it, where you choose a campaign objective, build ads made for that objective, control where they appear, test versions against each other and track what happens on your website or WhatsApp afterwards. If you only want more people to see a good post, a boost is fine. If you want enquiries, calls, messages or sales you can measure, use Ads Manager.

Both spend money with the same company and show ads in the same feeds. The difference is how much control you have over who sees the ad and what Meta tries to get them to do.

## The quick comparison

| | Boost post | Ads Manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where you start | A post on your Page or Instagram profile | A new campaign in Ads Manager |
| Setup time | A couple of minutes | Longer, more choices to make |
| Goals | A short list of simple goals | Full campaign objectives (awareness, traffic, engagement, leads, app promotion, sales) |
| The ad itself | Your existing post, as it is | Ads built for the goal, with your own headline, text and button |
| Audience | Basic targeting, or people Meta suggests | Detailed targeting, custom audiences from your own customers or website visitors, and lookalikes |
| Placements | Limited choice | Full control over Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and other placements |
| Testing | Hard to compare versions | Several ads and ad sets, A/B tests |
| Tracking | Mostly likes, reach, clicks, messages | Website enquiries and purchases through the Meta pixel and Conversions API |
| Best for | Getting a strong post seen locally | Steady, measurable enquiries and sales |

## What boosting actually does

When you boost, Meta turns your organic post into an ad. You usually choose a goal such as getting more engagement, more profile visits, more messages or more website visits (the exact list changes from time to time and differs a little between Facebook and Instagram). Then you set the audience, the daily budget and how long it runs.

The strength is speed. A shop owner who has just posted a festive offer can boost it from a phone in two minutes. The weakness is that the ad is the post. If the post was written to be friendly on your feed, not to make a stranger act, it often gets likes and very few enquiries. Likes feel like progress, but they do not pay the bills.

Boosted posts also show up inside Ads Manager after you create them, so you can see their results there. You just could not set most of the options there when you created them.

## What Ads Manager gives you that boosting does not

### An objective that matches what you want

In Ads Manager you start by choosing the outcome: awareness, traffic, engagement, leads, app promotion or sales. Meta then shows the ad to people it expects are most likely to do that thing. If you want enquiries, a leads objective goes looking for people who fill in forms or start chats. An engagement boost goes looking for people who like and comment, who are often not the same people who buy.

### Ads written for strangers

You can write a headline, the main text and a call to action button such as Send WhatsApp Message, Call Now or Learn More, and use images or video made for the ad. You can run two or three versions at once and let the numbers show which one works.

### Your own audiences

Ads Manager lets you build audiences from people who visited your website, people who messaged you, or a customer list you upload, and then find people similar to them. For a business that already has customers, this is often where the best results come from.

### Tracking past the click

If your ad sends people to your website, the [Meta pixel](https://www.facebook.com/business/tools/meta-pixel) and the Conversions API tell Meta which clicks turned into enquiries or purchases. Meta then optimises for more of those, not just more clicks. A boost that sends people to your site rarely gets set up this way, so it cannot learn which visitors were valuable.

### Lead forms and WhatsApp ads done properly

Ads Manager is where you set up instant lead forms with qualifying questions, or ads that open a WhatsApp chat with a ready message. We compare the two main options in [Meta lead form vs landing page](/insights/meta-lead-form-vs-landing-page).

## When boosting a post is fine

- **The post is already doing well** without paying, and you want more local people to see it.
- **You want awareness, not enquiries**, for example a new branch opening, an event or a seasonal greeting.
- **The budget is small and short**, such as a few days around a festival.
- **You have no website or tracking yet** and simply want more people to discover your profile.
- **You are learning** and want to see how paid reach works before setting up proper campaigns.

## When you should move to Ads Manager

- **You want enquiries, calls, WhatsApp messages or sales**, and you want to know what each one cost.
- **You are spending every month**, not just once in a while. Regular spend deserves proper setup.
- **Your boosts get likes but no business.** This is the most common sign.
- **You sell something high value**, such as a home interior project, a clinic treatment, a course or a machine, where the ad needs to explain and filter.
- **You have a website and customer list** that Meta could learn from.
- **You want to run the same offer to new people and to past visitors** with different messages.

## How to switch without wasting money

1. **Write down one goal.** For example, "WhatsApp enquiries for home cleaning in Adajan and Vesu." One campaign, one goal.
2. **Set up tracking first.** Install the Meta pixel on your website, and if you can, the Conversions API. For WhatsApp ads, make sure your WhatsApp Business number is connected to your Page.
3. **Pick the matching objective.** Leads for forms and chats, sales for online orders, traffic only if you genuinely just want visits.
4. **Make two or three ads**, each with a clear offer, a real photo or short video, and one button.
5. **Start with a budget you can keep running** for at least a couple of weeks. Changing settings every day stops Meta from learning.
6. **Reply fast.** Messages and leads go cold within hours. Someone has to answer every one.
7. **Judge by cost per real enquiry and per customer**, not by reach or likes.

## Mistakes we often see

- **Boosting every post.** It spreads a small budget thin across content that was never meant to sell.
- **Using the engagement goal when you want enquiries.** Meta will get you exactly what you asked for: engagement.
- **Sending ads to the homepage** instead of a page about the exact offer.
- **No one checking messages** on Instagram, Messenger or WhatsApp while ads are running.
- **Targeting the whole country** when you only serve one city.
- **Stopping after three days** because results look slow, before Meta has had time to learn.

## FAQ

**Is boosting a post a waste of money?** Not always. It works for getting a good post seen by more people nearby. It is a poor tool for steady enquiries, because the goals, ad format and tracking are limited.

**Is Ads Manager more expensive than boosting?** No. You set the budget in both. Ads Manager does not cost more per day; it gives you more ways to spend the same money on the people most likely to act.

**Can I boost from Instagram too?** Yes. You can boost eligible posts and reels from the Instagram app on a professional account, and the ads run through the same Meta ad system.

**Do I need a Facebook Page to run ads in Ads Manager?** Ads run from a Facebook Page or a connected Instagram account, through an ad account. Most small businesses set this up once in Meta Business Suite.

**How much should I spend to start?** There is no single right number. Start with an amount you can keep running for a few weeks without stopping, then look at the cost per real enquiry before increasing it.

**Should I run Google Ads instead?** They do different jobs. Google Ads reach people already searching for what you sell, while Meta ads reach people who are not searching yet. We explain how to choose a starting point in [Google Ads vs SEO](/insights/google-ads-vs-seo-for-small-business).
