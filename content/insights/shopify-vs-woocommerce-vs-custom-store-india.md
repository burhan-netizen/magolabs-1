If you want to start selling online quickly with very little technical work, **Shopify** is usually the easiest choice, but you pay a monthly plan fee and, because Shopify's own payment system is not available in India, an extra fee on every online order. **WooCommerce** is free software that runs on a WordPress website you host yourself, so it is cheaper to run but needs more care. A **custom store** costs more upfront, but it is built around your products, your buyers and the way you actually take orders. For a small catalogue and a fast start, pick Shopify. For a tight budget and someone to maintain it, pick WooCommerce. For B2B ordering, unusual pricing, or a brand that needs to stand apart, a custom store is often the better long-term fit.

Here is how the three compare for a business in Surat, across Gujarat, or anywhere in India.

## The quick comparison

| | Shopify | WooCommerce | Custom store |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | A hosted platform you rent monthly | A free plugin for WordPress, on hosting you pay for | A store built for your business by a developer or agency |
| Upfront cost | Low, unless you pay for a premium theme or setup | Low to medium, depending on theme and setup | Highest of the three |
| Running cost | Monthly plan, paid apps, plus a per-order fee with Indian payment gateways | Hosting, paid extensions, updates and backups | Hosting and support, with no platform fee per order |
| Technical work for you | Least | Most, unless someone maintains it for you | Little, if your developer handles updates |
| Flexibility | Good within Shopify's limits | High, with plugins and code | Complete |
| Best for | D2C brands that want to launch fast | Budget stores with someone technical on hand | B2B sellers, manufacturers, brands with special needs |

## Shopify: fast to launch, but watch the fees

Shopify hosts everything for you: the store, security, checkout and updates. You pick a theme, add products, connect a payment gateway and start selling. For a new D2C brand selling clothes, sarees, jewellery or packaged food, it can get you live quickly.

What Indian business owners should know before signing up:

- **Shopify Payments is not available in India.** It is not on Shopify's list of [supported countries](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries), so Indian stores use a third-party gateway such as Razorpay, PayU or Cashfree.
- **That means a third-party transaction fee.** When you use an outside payment provider, Shopify charges its own fee on each order, on top of what your gateway charges. On Shopify's [pricing page](https://www.shopify.com/pricing) this is currently 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow and 0.6% on Advanced. Shopify's [billing help page](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/pricing-plans/pricing-overview) notes that manual payment methods, such as cash on delivery, are not charged this fee.
- **Apps add up.** Reviews, WhatsApp order alerts, GST invoices, wishlists and advanced filters are often paid apps, each with its own monthly charge.
- **You rent, you do not own.** If you stop paying, the store goes offline. You can export your products and customers, but the design and setup stay with Shopify.

Shopify makes the most sense when speed matters more than control and your order value is high enough that the per-order fee does not hurt your margin.

## WooCommerce: low running cost, more responsibility

WooCommerce turns a WordPress website into a store. The plugin itself is free, and you pay for hosting, a domain, a theme and any paid extensions you need. Indian payment gateways such as Razorpay offer WooCommerce plugins, and there is no platform fee on each order.

The trade off is upkeep:

- **Updates are your job.** WordPress, WooCommerce, the theme and every plugin need regular updates. Skipping them can break the site or leave it open to hacking.
- **Speed depends on your hosting.** Cheap shared hosting can make a store slow, especially with many product photos. Slow stores lose buyers on mobile.
- **Plugins can clash.** Ten plugins from ten developers do not always work well together, and troubleshooting takes time.
- **Backups and security are not automatic** unless your host or developer sets them up.

WooCommerce suits a business that wants low running costs and has a developer or agency who will maintain it properly.

## Custom store: built around how you actually sell

A custom store is designed and built for your business rather than adapted from a template. It costs more at the start, but there is no platform charging you a percentage of each sale, and the store can do exactly what your business needs.

A custom build is usually worth it when:

- **You sell B2B or wholesale.** Minimum order quantities, price slabs by quantity, separate prices for dealers, or "request a quote" instead of a cart are awkward on off the shelf platforms.
- **Your catalogue is unusual.** Fabric sold by the metre, diamonds with certificates and many attributes, or products made to order need custom filters and product pages.
- **You need it to connect to your own systems,** such as your billing software, ERP or warehouse.
- **Your brand needs to look different.** Thousands of stores use the same popular themes. A custom design helps you look like an established business, not a template.
- **You want speed and SEO under control.** A store built lean, without stacks of plugins or apps, is easier to keep fast and well structured for Google.

## What every Indian online store needs, whichever you choose

The platform matters less than getting these basics right:

1. **A working Indian payment gateway** with UPI, cards and net banking, plus cash on delivery if your buyers expect it.
2. **Clear policy pages.** Payment gateways generally ask for terms, privacy, refund and contact pages before they activate your account.
3. **Seller details shown on the site.** India's Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 expect online sellers to show who they are, how to contact them, and how returns, refunds and complaints work.
4. **GST ready invoices** if you are GST registered.
5. **Shipping that is set up properly,** with a courier or aggregator, pin code checks and clear delivery times.
6. **A fast mobile experience.** Most of your buyers will shop on their phones.
7. **An easy way to ask questions.** A visible WhatsApp or call button helps buyers who want to check size, colour or delivery before paying.
8. **Product pages that can rank,** with real descriptions, not copied manufacturer text, so people can find you on Google.

## How to decide in five questions

1. **How soon do you need to sell?** If it is this month and the catalogue is simple, Shopify is the fastest start.
2. **What is your average order value and margin?** If margins are thin, a per-order platform fee adds up, which favours WooCommerce or custom.
3. **Who will maintain the store?** If no one technical will handle updates, avoid a self managed WooCommerce setup.
4. **Do you sell B2B, wholesale or made to order?** If yes, a custom store usually fits better.
5. **How many products, and how often do they change?** Every option handles small catalogues. Large, attribute heavy catalogues need careful planning on any platform.

## FAQ

**Is Shopify cheaper than a custom website in India?** At the start, usually yes. Over a few years, the monthly plan, paid apps and per-order fees can add up to more than a custom build, especially as sales grow. Compare the total cost over two or three years, not just the first month.

**Can I use Razorpay with Shopify and WooCommerce?** Yes. Razorpay, and other Indian gateways such as PayU and Cashfree, offer integrations for both. On Shopify, the third-party transaction fee still applies to orders paid online.

**Is WooCommerce really free?** The plugin is free. Hosting, your domain, a good theme, paid extensions and someone to maintain the store are not.

**Which is best for SEO?** All three can rank well. What matters more is fast pages, unique product descriptions, a clear category structure and a well looked after [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) if you also sell locally.

**Can I move from Shopify or WooCommerce to a custom store later?** Yes. Products and customer data can be exported and imported. Plan redirects from old product addresses to new ones so you do not lose your Google rankings.
