For most small business websites in India (a company site with service pages, a contact form and a WhatsApp button), **good shared hosting or a managed cloud host is enough**. You only need a VPS or a dedicated cloud server when your site gets heavy traffic, runs custom software, or sells online with a large catalogue. What matters more than the hosting "type" is **speed for Indian visitors, free SSL, daily backups, honest renewal prices, and the account being in your own name**.

Hosting is one of those things business owners buy once, forget about, and only think about again when the site goes down or the renewal bill arrives. Here is how to get it right the first time.

## What hosting actually is

Your website is a set of files (and often a database) that has to live on a computer that is switched on and connected to the internet all day, every day. Hosting is renting space on that computer. Your **domain** (like yourbusiness.in) is the address; **hosting** is the building at that address. They are separate things, often bought from separate companies, and you can move one without the other. If you are still choosing a domain, read [.in vs .com for an Indian business](/insights/in-vs-com-domain-for-indian-business).

## The main types of hosting, in plain terms

| Type | What it means | Good for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shared hosting | Your site shares one server with many other sites | Small business sites, low to moderate traffic | Slowdowns if a neighbour site uses too many resources; "unlimited" plans with hidden limits |
| VPS (virtual private server) | A guaranteed slice of a server, with its own resources | Growing sites, custom apps, stores with steady traffic | Someone has to manage security updates and server settings |
| Cloud hosting | Your site runs on a network of servers that can scale up when needed | Sites with traffic spikes, modern websites, stores | Pricing can be usage based and harder to predict |
| Managed hosting | The host handles updates, security, backups and speed for you | Owners who don't want to touch technical settings | Costs more than basic shared hosting; may limit plugins or settings |
| Dedicated server | An entire physical server just for you | Large portals and high traffic applications | Expensive and overkill for almost every small business |

These categories overlap. "Managed WordPress hosting" can run on cloud servers, and many modern websites are served from a global network (a CDN) that keeps copies of your pages close to visitors.

## Which one does your business need?

- **A brochure or company website** (5 to 20 pages, enquiry form, WhatsApp): reliable shared hosting or managed cloud hosting is plenty.
- **A website that publishes a lot of content or runs ads to it**: managed or cloud hosting, so the site stays fast when traffic jumps after a campaign.
- **An online store**: start with managed or cloud hosting. If you use a hosted platform like Shopify, the hosting is part of the platform. We compared the options in [Shopify vs WooCommerce vs a custom store](/insights/shopify-vs-woocommerce-vs-custom-store-india).
- **A portal, booking system or custom software** with logins and a busy database: a VPS or a properly sized cloud server, with someone responsible for maintaining it.

If you are unsure, start smaller with a host that lets you upgrade without moving the site. Paying for a big server "just in case" is one of the most common ways small businesses waste money.

## Why speed and server location matter

Google measures real visitor experience through **Core Web Vitals**. According to Google's [web.dev guidance](https://web.dev/articles/vitals), a good experience means the main content loads within **2.5 seconds** (Largest Contentful Paint), the page responds to taps within **200 milliseconds** (Interaction to Next Paint), and the layout doesn't jump around (Cumulative Layout Shift under **0.1**).

Hosting is only one part of speed, since heavy images and bloated themes slow sites more often than servers do. But a slow or overloaded server makes every page wait before anything else can happen. For a business whose customers are in Surat, Gujarat or the rest of India, choose hosting with servers in India or a CDN with Indian locations. If your buyers are in the UK, US or Australia, make sure your pages are also served quickly there.

Speed also affects enquiries directly. Most of your visitors are on mobile data, and a page that takes too long to load often gets closed before the contact button even appears. ## The checklist before you pay for hosting

1. **Free SSL (https).** Every site should load over https. Free certificates, such as those from Let's Encrypt, are widely supported, so you should not pay extra for a basic one.
2. **Automatic daily backups**, kept for at least a couple of weeks, and an easy way to restore them. Ask who restores the site if something breaks.
3. **Renewal price, not the first year offer.** Many plans advertise a low first year price on a long term, then renew at a much higher rate. Check the renewal price before you buy.
4. **Real resource limits.** "Unlimited" plans still have limits on processing power, memory or files. Read the fair use policy.
5. **Uptime and support.** Look for a published uptime commitment and support that answers quickly, ideally by chat or phone.
6. **Business email.** Decide whether email (like you@yourbusiness.in) will run on your hosting or a separate email service. Separate email means a website problem doesn't take your email down with it.
7. **Account ownership.** The hosting account should be in your name, with your email, and you should have the login. More on this below.

## The ownership trap

We regularly meet business owners whose website hosting was bought by a previous developer, on the developer's account, with the developer's card. When that person stops answering calls, the business can't renew, can't move the site and sometimes can't even access its own email. In the worst cases the site simply disappears when the hosting expires.

Protect yourself:

- Keep the **domain and hosting registered in your business name**, with an email address you control.
- Make sure you have the **logins**, or at least admin access, stored somewhere safe.
- Ask for a **full copy of the website files** and database, or confirm you can download a backup yourself.
- Turn on **auto renew** for both domain and hosting so nothing lapses by accident.

## Do you have to pay for hosting separately?

Not always. Some website builders and agencies include hosting in their price. When comparing quotes, check whether hosting is included, for how long, and what the yearly renewal will be. A cheap website with expensive yearly hosting and maintenance can cost more over three years than a higher quote with hosting included.

## FAQ

**Can I move my website to a different host later?** Yes. Your files and database are copied to the new host and the domain is pointed to it. Done carefully, visitors and Google rankings are not affected. It is much easier if you own the hosting and domain accounts.

**Does hosting affect SEO?** Indirectly. A fast, reliable host helps your pages load quickly and stay online, which matters for visitors and for Google. A site that is often down or very slow can lose rankings and enquiries.

**Is free hosting good enough for a business?** Usually not. Free plans often come with ads, limited support, a subdomain instead of your own domain, or no backups. For a business that depends on enquiries, it is a false saving.

**Should my hosting be in India?** If most customers are in India, servers in India or a CDN with Indian locations will help speed. If you serve several countries, a CDN matters more than where the main server sits.

**What is the difference between hosting and a domain?** The domain is your address; hosting is where the website actually lives. You need both, and both should be in your name.
