The most reliable way to get more Google reviews is simple: **ask every customer, at the moment they are happiest, and send them your direct review link** so leaving a review takes ten seconds. Google gives every Business Profile a review link and a QR code for exactly this. What you must not do is pay for reviews, offer a discount in return, or only ask the customers you expect to be happy. Google's policies prohibit all three, and breaking them can cost you the reviews you already have.

Reviews matter for two reasons. They are one of the signals Google uses to decide which businesses appear in local results, and they are what people read before they decide to call you. A clinic with 150 recent, detailed reviews looks very different to a buyer than one with 9 reviews from three years ago.

## Step 1: Get your review link and QR code

Google creates both for you. On a computer, open your Business Profile, select **Read reviews**, then **Get more reviews**. You can copy the link or save the QR code as an image. Google notes that the QR code can currently only be generated from a computer browser, not from a phone. The full steps are on Google's help page, [Create a Google link or QR code to request reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815).

Save the link somewhere you can paste it quickly, such as a WhatsApp quick reply or a note on your phone. The easier it is for you to send, the more often you will actually send it.

## Step 2: Put the link where customers already are

| Where | How to use it |
| --- | --- |
| WhatsApp | Send a short thank-you message with the link after the job, visit or delivery |
| Counter or reception | A small printed card or standee with the QR code |
| Invoices and receipts | Print the QR code at the bottom with one line asking for feedback |
| Email | Add the link to your follow-up or delivery email and your signature |
| Packaging | A small insert card for product businesses and online orders |
| Your website | A "Review us on Google" link on your contact or thank-you page |

WhatsApp usually works best for Indian businesses because customers already read it and can tap the link straight away. If you use WhatsApp Business, save the request as a quick reply so anyone on your team can send it in two taps.

## Step 3: Ask at the right moment

Timing does more than wording. Ask when the customer has just felt the value of what you did:

- **Clinics:** after the treatment is complete and the patient feels better, not while they wait to be called in
- **Service businesses (CA firms, consultants, contractors):** right after a filing is done, a problem is solved or a project is handed over
- **Shops and showrooms:** at billing, or the same evening by WhatsApp
- **Manufacturers and B2B suppliers:** after a successful delivery or a repeat order, from the person who manages the relationship
- **Restaurants and cafes:** a QR code on the table or bill, with no pressure

Ask in person when you can, then follow up with the link. A personal "it would really help us if you shared your experience on Google" gets far more reviews than a generic automated message.

## Step 4: Use a short, honest message

Keep the request short and do not tell the customer what to write or what rating to give. For example:

> Thank you for choosing us today. If you have a minute, we would really value your honest feedback on Google. It helps other people find us. [your review link]

You can send it in English, Gujarati or Hindi, whichever the customer uses with you. Reviews in any language count.

## What Google does not allow

Google's [Maps user contributed content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) treats these as fake engagement. Content that breaks it can be removed:

- **Incentives of any kind.** No payment, discount, free product, free service or contest entry in exchange for a review, or for changing or removing a negative one.
- **Review gating.** You cannot selectively ask only the customers you expect to be happy, or send unhappy customers somewhere else first. Ask everyone the same way.
- **Discouraging negative reviews.** You cannot ask customers not to post a bad review.
- **Pressure on the premises.** Google says businesses should not require or pressure people to leave a rating or review while they are on site. Showing a QR code is fine; standing over someone until they post is not.
- **Scripting the content.** Do not ask customers to mention particular words, services or staff names.
- **Staff targets.** Google specifically mentions merchants asking staff to collect a set number of reviews, or reviews that name a staff member.
- **Reviewing yourself.** Reviews from owners, employees or accounts created by one person do not reflect a genuine customer experience.
- **Buying reviews** from agencies or "review packages". These are the easiest kind for Google to detect, and they are the fastest way to lose reviews you earned honestly.

What Google does allow is asking customers to share their genuine experience, without offering anything and without trying to influence the rating or what they write.

## How to reply to reviews

Replying shows future customers that a real person runs the business and cares. Reply to every review, good and bad, from your Business Profile.

**Good reviews:** thank the person by name and mention something specific when you can. Keep it to one or two lines.

**Bad reviews:**

1. **Wait until you are calm.** A defensive reply is read by everyone who visits your profile after.
2. **Acknowledge the experience** without arguing about details in public.
3. **Never share private details**, such as a patient's treatment or a client's tax matter. This matters especially for doctors and CA firms.
4. **Offer to fix it offline** with a phone number or WhatsApp contact.
5. **If you resolve it**, you can politely let the customer know they are welcome to update their review. Do not offer anything in return for changing it.

A sensible, polite reply to a bad review often builds more trust than the review takes away.

## Can you remove a bad review?

You cannot delete a review just because you do not like it. If a review breaks Google's policies, for example it is spam, off topic, from someone who was never a customer, from a competitor, or contains hate speech or personal information, you can report it from your Business Profile. Google reviews the report against its policy and decides. A genuine negative review from a real customer will normally stay, which is why a steady flow of new, positive reviews and good replies is the real answer.

## Mistakes that keep review counts low

- **Asking once, at launch, and never again.** Reviews need to keep coming. Recent reviews matter to customers more than old ones.
- **Sending people to search for your business** instead of giving them the direct link. Many give up.
- **Leaving it to one person.** Make the request part of how every job, visit or order ends.
- **Never replying,** so the profile looks unattended.
- **A profile with the wrong name, hours or phone number.** People who cannot find or trust your listing will not review it. Our guide to [why a business is not showing on Google Maps](/insights/why-is-my-business-not-showing-on-google-maps) covers the common causes.

## FAQ

**How many Google reviews does a business need?** There is no fixed number. What matters is having more recent, genuine reviews than the businesses you compete with in your area, and a steady flow of new ones over time.

**Can I offer a small discount for a review?** No. Google's policy prohibits discounts, free goods or services, or any other incentive in exchange for a review, even if you do not ask for a positive one.

**Can I ask friends and family to review my business?** Only if they are genuine customers with a real experience to describe. Reviews from people who have not used your business count as fake engagement.

**Do reviews help me rank on Google Maps?** Yes. Google lists prominence as one of the main factors in local results, and reviews and ratings are part of it. See our step-by-step guide on [how to rank on Google Maps in Surat](/insights/how-to-rank-on-google-maps-in-surat).

**Should I reply to old reviews?** Yes. Replying to older reviews is still worth doing, especially unanswered negative ones, because new visitors read them.

**Can I show my Google reviews on my website?** Yes, and it helps visitors trust you. Show real reviews as they appear, with the reviewer's name as shown on Google, and do not edit their words.
