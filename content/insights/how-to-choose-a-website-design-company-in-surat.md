The best way to choose a website design company in Surat is to ask the same set of direct questions to every option and compare the answers, not just the prices. Focus on who owns the domain and code, who writes the content, what is included, how long it takes, and what happens after launch. A good company will answer all of these clearly and in writing.

Surat has hundreds of freelancers, agencies and studios offering websites, and from the outside they all look similar. We are one of them, so take our advice with that in mind. These are the questions we would want our own family to ask before hiring anyone, including us.

## Who will own the domain, hosting and website code?

You should. The domain, hosting account and website files should be registered in your business name, with your email, and you should have the login details.

This is the single most important question. Some providers register the domain in their own name or keep the hosting under their account. Everything works fine until you want to switch providers, and then you find you cannot move your own website. Ask for this in writing. If they say "we will manage everything for you", that is fine, as long as the accounts are still yours.

## Is the website custom-designed or built on a template?

Ask directly whether your site will be designed and built for you, or based on a ready-made theme. Both are valid, but they are not the same product and should not be priced the same.

Templates are quicker and cheaper but look generic, and they often carry extra code that slows the site down. Custom sites cost more but are built around your business and your customers. We only build custom, hand-coded websites, but the important thing is that you know what you are paying for.

## Who writes the content for my website?

Find out whether the company writes your text, or expects you to provide it. This is where many projects get stuck for weeks.

Most business owners are experts in their work, not in writing web copy. If the company writes your content, ask whether it is written to bring enquiries (conversion copywriting) or just to fill space. Also ask about photos: will they use stock images, your photos, or arrange a shoot?

## How long will the website take, and what can delay it?

A clear timeline with milestones is a good sign. Typical business sites take 1 to 4 weeks, and ecommerce sites take longer.

Ask what the company needs from you and by when. Most delays come from waiting on content, approvals or logins. For reference, our packages have fixed timelines: Launch in 5 days, Growth in 14 days, Scale in 21 days and Commerce in 28 days.

## How many revisions are included?

Ask how many rounds of changes are included in the price, and what counts as a revision. Unlimited revisions sounds generous, but it often means the scope was never defined.

A good process has a design stage where you approve the look, followed by a build stage where you review the working site. Changing small text after launch should be easy. Redesigning the whole homepage after it is built is a new piece of work.

## What exactly is included in the price?

Ask for a written scope listing every page, feature and deliverable. If two quotes are ₹20,000 apart, the scope usually explains why.

Check for: number of pages, contact forms, WhatsApp and click-to-call buttons, Google Maps embed, blog, booking or payment features, Google Analytics and Search Console setup, and training on how to update the site.

## Is basic SEO included?

Basic on-page SEO should be included in any professional website. That means proper page titles, meta descriptions, heading structure, image alt text, a sitemap, clean URLs, mobile-friendliness and Google Search Console setup.

Ongoing SEO (local SEO, content, link building) is a separate service, and that is fine. Be cautious of anyone who promises "page one of Google" in a fixed time. Nobody controls Google's rankings.

## How fast will my website load on a mobile phone?

Ask how they make sure the site loads quickly on mobile data, and ask to see their past sites tested on Google PageSpeed Insights.

Most of your visitors will be on a phone, often on patchy mobile data. A slow site loses people before they see anything. Heavy themes, large images and too many plugins are the usual causes.

## Can I see live websites you have built, and speak to past clients?

Yes, you should be able to. Ask for live links, not just screenshots or a Behance-style gallery.

Then verify: open the sites on your phone, check they load properly, and see whether the business actually exists. If possible, call one or two past clients and ask how the project went and whether the company still responds to them. Real results are worth asking about too. For example, we can point to a dental clinic that went from 25 to 58 patients a day within two months of launch, with appointment calls up 63%, and a CA firm, Darshan Galani & Co., that signed 34 new clients within three months of its first website.

## What are the payment terms?

A fair arrangement is a split payment: an advance to start and the balance on completion or before going live. Be careful with 100% upfront payment, especially with someone you have not worked with.

Also ask for a fixed quote before work begins, so the price does not grow halfway through. We give every client a fixed quote before we start for this reason.

## What happens after the website goes live?

Ask what support is included after launch, for how long, and what it costs after that. Bugs, small text changes and questions are normal in the first few weeks.

Also ask about the yearly costs: domain renewal, hosting, SSL, business email and any maintenance contract (AMC). Get the total for year two in writing, not just year one.

## Will I be able to update the website myself?

Ask whether you will be able to change text, photos, prices or blog posts yourself, or whether every change needs the company.

Neither answer is wrong, but you should know in advance. If you will need them for every update, ask how quickly they respond and whether small changes are charged.

## What are the red flags when choosing a web design company?

The clearest red flags are vague answers to the questions above. Specifically, watch for:

-   The domain or hosting will be in their name, not yours.
    
-   No written scope, just a single price.
    
-   A portfolio of screenshots with no live links.
    
-   Guaranteed Google rankings or "instant" traffic.
    
-   Very low prices with unclear renewal charges.
    
-   Pressure to pay fully upfront or decide today.
    
-   Slow or unclear replies before you have even paid. It rarely gets better after.
    

## FAQ

**Should I choose a local Surat company or a remote one?** Either can work. A local company is easier to meet in person and understands local customers. What matters most is clear communication, ownership and quality.

**Is the cheapest quote ever the right choice?** Sometimes, if the scope is the same and you own everything. Usually the cheapest quote leaves something out, such as content, SEO or support.

**How many companies should I get quotes from?** Two to four is enough. Send each the same brief so you can compare fairly.

**What should I prepare before contacting a website company?** Your goals for the site, a rough list of pages, examples of websites you like, your logo, and any content or photos you already have.

**Do I need to sign a contract?** Yes, or at least a written proposal that you both approve. It should cover scope, timeline, price, payment terms and ownership.
