A **Meta lead form** (Meta calls it an instant form) opens inside Facebook or Instagram with the person's name and phone number already filled in, so it usually brings **more leads at a lower cost per lead**, but a larger share of them are not serious. A **landing page** on your own website asks people to leave the app and fill in a form, so it brings **fewer leads that are usually warmer**, because only people who are genuinely interested make that extra effort. For a low-cost service or a quick callback offer, start with a lead form and tighten it. For a high-value service where people need to read, compare and trust you first, send ads to a landing page.

Both can work well. The real question is which kind of lead your sales team can turn into customers.

## The quick comparison

| | Meta lead form | Landing page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it opens | Inside Facebook or Instagram | On your own website |
| Effort for the person | A few taps, details prefilled | Leave the app, read, type details |
| Lead volume | Usually higher | Usually lower |
| Lead quality | Mixed, many casual taps | Usually more serious |
| What you can show | A short intro, a few questions | Full details, photos, prices, proof, FAQs |
| Tracking needed | Works inside Meta | Needs the Meta pixel or Conversions API set up |
| Who owns the page | Meta | You |
| Best for | Simple offers, callbacks, events, quick quotes | High-value services, B2B, anything that needs trust first |

## How a Meta lead form works

When someone taps your ad, a form slides up without leaving the app. Meta fills in details it already has, such as name, email and phone number, so submitting can take a few seconds. You can add your own questions, a short intro screen and a thank-you screen with a button to call you, visit your website or message you.

That convenience is the strength and the weakness. People tap through quickly, sometimes by accident, sometimes out of curiosity, and sometimes with an old phone number on their profile. When the business calls the next day, the person may not remember filling in anything.

Ads Manager lets you choose a form type when you create the form. At the time of writing, the options include one built for **more volume** and one built for **higher intent**, which adds a step where the person reviews and confirms their details before submitting. Meta renames and moves these settings from time to time, so check the current options in Ads Manager and Meta's own [lead ads overview](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/lead-ads).

## How a landing page works

The ad sends people to a page on your website built for that one offer. A good landing page has one clear headline that matches the ad, a short explanation of what you offer and who it is for, proof such as real photos of your work or genuine reviews, answers to common questions, and an easy way to enquire: a short form, a call button and a [WhatsApp button](/insights/add-whatsapp-button-to-website).

Because the person has to open a web page and type their details, fewer people finish. But the ones who do have read about you, seen what you do and still chosen to contact you. That is a warmer lead.

The landing page has to be fast on mobile. Most Meta traffic comes from phones, often on mobile data, and a page that takes too long to load loses people before they read a word. You also need tracking set up (the Meta pixel and ideally the Conversions API) so Meta knows which clicks turned into enquiries and can find more people like them.

## When a lead form makes more sense

- **Your offer is simple and easy to understand** in one or two lines, like a free trial class, a home visit, a site inspection or a callback.
- **You can call leads back within minutes or hours.** Lead form enquiries go cold fast.
- **You do not have a good landing page yet** and need enquiries now.
- **The price is low enough** that people do not need to research much before enquiring.
- **You are running an event or a limited-time offer** where speed matters more than depth.

## When a landing page makes more sense

- **Your service is high value**, such as a home interior project, a factory machine, a clinic treatment or a professional service, where people want details before they talk to anyone.
- **You sell to other businesses**, where buyers compare suppliers and check your credibility.
- **Your sales team is small** and cannot afford to spend hours calling people who are not serious.
- **You want to use the same page for Google Ads too.** One good landing page can serve both, which we explain in [Google Ads vs SEO](/insights/google-ads-vs-seo-for-small-business).
- **You need to explain prices, process or eligibility** before someone enquires, so the wrong people filter themselves out.

## How to get better leads from a Meta lead form

If lead forms bring volume but too many junk enquiries, change the form before you give up on it.

1. **Use the higher intent form type** so people confirm their details before submitting.
2. **Add one or two qualifying questions** with multiple choice answers, such as budget range, location or timeline. Each extra question lowers volume a little and raises quality.
3. **Ask for something the person must type**, such as their area or what they need. Prefilled fields alone make it too easy to tap through.
4. **Be clear about price or minimum order** in the ad or intro screen if it filters out the wrong people.
5. **Call or WhatsApp within the hour.** A lead contacted the same day is far more likely to remember you.
6. **Connect the form to your CRM or a sheet** so leads do not sit unseen in Ads Manager. Download or sync them regularly.
7. **Tell Meta which leads were good.** If you can send back which leads became customers, Meta can optimise for quality instead of just quantity.

## A third option: ads that open WhatsApp

For many Indian businesses, an ad that opens a WhatsApp chat works better than either. The person taps, a chat opens with a ready message, and your team replies. There is no form to fill and you get a real conversation from the first message. It suits businesses where customers like to ask questions before booking, such as clinics, coaching classes, showrooms and service providers.

The catch is that someone has to reply quickly, every time. If chats wait for hours, this option wastes money faster than a form does. We compare WhatsApp setups in [WhatsApp Business App vs API](/insights/whatsapp-business-app-vs-api).

## Mistakes that waste ad money

- **Judging only by cost per lead.** A cheaper lead that never answers the phone costs more than a pricier lead that buys.
- **Sending ads to your homepage** instead of a page about the exact offer in the ad.
- **No tracking on the landing page**, so Meta cannot learn which clicks became enquiries.
- **Leaving leads uncalled for a day or more.** Lead form leads cool off quickly.
- **A form with only prefilled fields**, which invites accidental and casual submissions.
- **Changing everything at once**, so you cannot tell what improved results.

## How to decide

Run a simple test. Put the same offer and the same audience into two ad sets, one with a higher intent lead form and one pointing to a focused landing page. Give both enough budget and time to collect a fair number of leads. Then compare not just cost per lead, but how many leads answered, how many were genuine, and how many became customers. That last number is the one that matters.

## FAQ

**Are Facebook lead form leads fake?** Most are real people, but many are casual or accidental, and some profiles have old phone numbers. A higher intent form, qualifying questions and fast follow-up remove much of the problem.

**Do lead forms work on Instagram too?** Yes. Meta lead ads can run on both Facebook and Instagram placements from the same campaign in Ads Manager.

**Is a landing page the same as my website?** It lives on your website, but it is a page built for one offer, with one goal: getting the enquiry. Your homepage tries to do many things at once, which is why ads sent there usually perform worse.

**Which is cheaper?** Lead forms usually have a lower cost per lead. Landing pages often have a lower cost per customer when your service needs trust and explanation. Track both numbers.

**Can I use both at the same time?** Yes, and many businesses do. Use lead forms for simple offers and retargeting, and landing pages for high-value services and colder audiences.
