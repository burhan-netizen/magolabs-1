A website for an IT or ERP consulting firm has to **prove real expertise to a sceptical buyer**. Enterprise clients compare several consultants before they commit. They look for named technologies, clearly defined services, evidence of how you work, and an easy way to book a first conversation.

**Astrabizz Consultancy** works on ERP, CRM and AI-driven digital transformation projects, and regularly pitches against established firms for bigger clients. We designed their website to hold its own in that company. Here is how, and what any consulting firm can use from it.

## The problem: competing with bigger names

In consulting, trust is everything. Enterprise buyers are used to polished websites from large consulting firms. A smaller firm with a thin or generic site gets mentally filed as "a freelancer with a laptop", however strong its actual work is.

Astrabizz needed a website that made its depth of expertise obvious, so prospects would take it as seriously as the bigger names on their shortlist.

## What a consulting firm website needs

### 1. Specific technologies, not vague claims

"Digital transformation solutions" means nothing to a buyer. Naming the actual platforms you work with, and what you do with them, means a lot. The Astrabizz site clearly shows the **specific technologies** the firm works with, so an IT head can see at a glance whether the expertise matches their stack.

### 2. A page for each service

ERP implementation, CRM setup and AI projects attract different buyers with different questions. **Dedicated service pages** let each buyer go straight to what matters to them, and give Google clear pages to match against specific searches.

### 3. The expert behind the firm

Clients buy consultants, not logos. The site puts the founder's expertise up front, positioning Astrabizz as a **credible digital transformation partner**. When the buyer knows who they will actually work with, the first call starts on firmer ground.

### 4. Every page leads to a consultation

Enterprise sales start with a conversation. Every page on the site moves a sceptical visitor towards **booking a consultation**, with the call to action always within reach rather than hidden at the bottom of one page.

### 5. Serious, clean design

A consulting firm sells judgement. A clean, calm, well-organised site suggests a well-organised consultancy. Clutter, stock photos and buzzwords suggest the opposite.

## The result

Astrabizz now has a website that reflects the seriousness of its work, and gives the founder a genuine edge when pitching to bigger companies that are evaluating several consulting partners. In his words, enquiries now come **directly through the site**, and prospects arrive at the first conversation already knowing what Astrabizz offers.

## A quick checklist for your consulting website

- Does it name the specific platforms and technologies you work with?
- Does each service have its own clear page?
- Is it obvious who the expert behind the firm is?
- Can a visitor book a consultation from any page?
- Would it stand up next to the biggest firm on a client's shortlist?

## Common mistakes on consulting firm websites

**Buzzwords instead of specifics.** "Innovative digital solutions" says nothing. Name the platforms you work with and the problems you solve.

**No clear service structure.** A single page covering ERP, CRM, AI and support leaves buyers unsure what you actually focus on. Give each service its own space.

**Hiding the experts.** Enterprise buyers want to know who will run their project. A site with no names, roles or experience feels like a reseller, not a partner.

**No route to a conversation.** If booking a consultation means hunting for a contact page, many buyers will not bother. Put the next step on every page.

**A cluttered design.** A consultancy sells clear thinking. A crowded, busy website suggests the opposite.

**Nothing for the second reader.** The person who visits your site often forwards it to a manager. Make sure the site explains your offer to someone who has never spoken to you.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should an ERP consultant's website include?

The platforms you implement, the industries you serve, a page for each main service, the experts behind the firm, and a clear way to book a consultation.

### How can a smaller consulting firm compete with big firms online?

By being specific. Name your technologies, explain your approach clearly and show who the client will actually work with. Large firms often hide behind generic copy, which gives a focused firm a real edge.

### Should consulting firms show case studies on their website?

Yes, where clients allow it. Even a short description of a project, the problem and the outcome helps a buyer picture working with you.

### How do we get more consultation bookings from our website?

Put a clear booking option on every page, keep the first step simple, and make sure visitors understand what happens after they book.
