If your Google Business Profile has been suspended, **do not create a new profile for the same business**. First find and fix whatever breaks Google's guidelines, then submit **one appeal through the Google Business Profile appeals tool**, with documents that show your business name and address are real, such as a registration certificate, licence, tax certificate or a utility bill. Google reviews the appeal and emails you the decision. Most suspensions come from a handful of fixable problems: extra keywords in the business name, an address that is not a real place customers can visit, or duplicate profiles.

A suspended profile is a serious problem for a local business. While it is suspended, customers cannot find you on Google Maps or in the local results, and you lose the calls, direction requests and reviews that come with it. The good news is that a genuine business that fixes the issue and appeals properly usually has a fair chance of getting its profile back.

## What a suspension actually means

Google can suspend or disable a Business Profile that does not follow its [guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177). According to Google's help page on [fixing suspended or disabled profiles](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145):

- When a profile is removed, the public cannot see it and the owner and managers cannot act on it.
- If the owner's Google Account itself is removed, all the locations that account owns are removed too.
- If a manager's account is removed, that manager loses access, but the locations are not affected.

There is also a separate, bigger problem: an **account restriction**. If your Google Account is restricted for breaking policy, the Business Profiles you manage are suspended and you cannot create or claim others. In that case you have to appeal the account restriction first, from the restrictions page in your Google Account, and only after that is lifted can you appeal the profile suspension.

## The most common reasons profiles get suspended

Google does not always spell out the exact reason, but the appeals tool shows the decision and links to the policy it relates to. These are the issues we see most often when business owners in Surat and across India ask us why their profile was suspended.

| Problem | What it looks like | How to fix it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keywords in the business name | "Shah Dental Clinic Best Dentist in Adajan Surat" | Use your real name exactly as it appears on your signboard, letterhead and registration |
| Address customers cannot visit | A virtual office, a shared co-working desk with no staff, a P.O. box or a relative's flat | Use the real place where you serve customers, or set up as a service-area business and hide the address |
| Duplicate profiles | Two or three profiles for the same business at the same address | Keep one, and ask Google to remove or merge the others |
| Ineligible business type | An online-only business with no in-person contact, or a rental or for-sale property listed as its own business | Only list a business that meets customers in person at a location or at the customer's place |
| Service-area business showing a home address | A plumber or AC repair service showing a residential address customers do not visit | Hide the address and set service areas instead |
| Sudden big edits | Changing the name, address and category all at once soon after verification | Make one change at a time and keep it consistent with your signage and website |
| Policy-breaking content | Fake reviews, paid reviews or misleading photos | Remove the cause and stop the practice before appealing |

### Keyword stuffing in the name

This is the single most common reason. Many profiles add city names, services or words like "best" and "No. 1" to the business name, hoping to rank higher. Google's guidelines say the name should match the real-world name of the business as used consistently on its storefront, website and stationery. Extra words are a policy violation, and competitors can and do suggest edits or report them.

### Address problems

Google expects the address on a profile to be a real location where you meet customers during your stated hours. Virtual offices and unstaffed addresses are not allowed. If you visit customers instead of having them come to you, such as a pest control service, a tutor who visits homes or a home salon, you should be set up as a **service-area business** with your address hidden and your service areas listed.

### Duplicates

Creating a second profile because the first one had a problem is a very common mistake, and it usually makes things worse. Google's help page is clear: do not create a new Business Profile for the same business while you appeal.

## How to appeal, step by step

The steps below follow Google's current help page. You need to be signed in to the Google Account that owns or manages the profile.

1. **Read the guidelines and fix the profile first.** Remove keywords from the name, correct the address or switch to a service-area setup, and make sure your category, phone number and website are accurate. Appeals can only be submitted once, so fix everything before you start.
2. **Gather your evidence.** Google lists official business registration, a business licence, tax certificates and utility bills (electricity, phone, water or internet) as evidence that can help. In India that could include your GST registration certificate, Udyam registration, shop and establishment licence, or a professional registration for clinics and CA firms. Google's tip is to check that the **business name and address on every document match the profile exactly**.
3. **Open the [Google Business Profile appeals tool](https://support.google.com/business/?p=manage_appeals)** and confirm the right Google Account.
4. **Select the profile, then the decision you want to appeal.** The tool shows the date of the decision, the reason, a link to the policy, whether it can be appealed and its current status.
5. **Attach your documents and explain your case.** Use **Choose file** to upload evidence (you can add more than one file), and use the text box to explain briefly and factually why the profile should be reinstated.
6. **Submit.** Tick the box confirming your evidence is attached and select **Submit appeal**. Google reviews it and sends the decision by email.

You can check progress in the same tool. The status shows as Submitted, Approved, Not Approved, Can't be appealed or Eligible for appeal.

### What to write in the appeal

Keep it short, honest and specific. A good explanation covers:

- What your business does and where customers are served
- What you have corrected, for example "removed the words 'best CA in Surat' from the business name"
- What the attached documents show
- That you have one profile for this location

Avoid long emotional messages, threats or claims you cannot back up. The person reviewing your appeal needs evidence, not pressure.

## If your appeal is denied

If the appeal is not approved, Google may allow an additional review through its [request for an additional review](https://support.google.com/business/contact/local_appeals) form, where you can add evidence that was not included the first time. Before you use it, look again at the policy linked in the decision and ask honestly whether the profile still breaks it. A second request with the same problem and the same documents is unlikely to change the outcome.

If you manage more than 10 suspended profiles, for example a chain of showrooms or clinics, the appeals tool offers a bulk reinstatement option where you attach a spreadsheet of profile IDs and supporting documents.

## How to avoid a suspension in the first place

- **Use your real business name**, the one on your board and your GST certificate, and nothing more.
- **Keep one profile per location**, and never create a new one to get around a problem.
- **Use a real, staffed address** or hide it if you are a service-area business.
- **Make major edits one at a time**, and keep your name, address and phone number the same on your profile, website and social pages.
- **Never buy reviews or offer anything in return for them.** Our guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/insights/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) covers what Google allows.
- **Control who has access.** Only add managers you trust, and remove old staff or past agencies who no longer work with you.

## FAQ

**How do I know if my Google Business Profile is suspended?** You will usually see a notice when you open your profile on Google Search or Maps, and Google may email the account owner. The appeals tool lists any decisions on your profile and their status.

**Should I create a new profile while the old one is suspended?** No. Google specifically says not to create a new Business Profile for the same business. A duplicate can make reinstatement harder.

**Can I appeal more than once?** An appeal through the appeals tool can only be submitted once per decision, so fix everything and attach all your evidence before you submit. If it is denied, Google may allow an additional review with new evidence.

**Will I lose my reviews?** Reviews are not visible while the profile is suspended. When Google reinstates a profile, it reinstates the profile itself along with its owner and managers.

**Can I get my profile back if I work from home?** Yes, if your business is eligible. If customers do not visit your home, set up as a service-area business, hide the address and list the areas you serve.

**Can an agency get my profile reinstated faster?** No one can skip Google's review. What an experienced team can do is find the actual cause, fix the profile properly and prepare a clean appeal with the right documents, which gives you the best chance the first time.
