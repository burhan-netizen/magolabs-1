**Google Ads** puts your business at the top of Google within a day or two, but you pay for every click and the enquiries stop the moment you stop paying. **SEO** takes a few months to show results, but once your pages rank, the visits keep coming without a per-click cost. For most small businesses the answer is not one or the other: use Google Ads when you need enquiries now or want to test what sells, and build SEO at the same time so you rely less on ads over the next year.

The right mix depends on your budget, how urgent your need for leads is, and how competitive your industry is in your city. Here is how to decide.

## The quick comparison

| | Google Ads | SEO |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How fast it works | Within a day or two of approval | Usually a few months |
| How you pay | Per click, on top of any management fee | Time and work on your website and profile |
| What happens when you stop | Ads disappear the same day | Rankings fade slowly, if at all |
| Where you appear | Marked "Sponsored" above or below normal results | In normal results and, for local searches, on Google Maps |
| Best for | Urgent leads, new launches, seasonal offers, testing | Long-term, steady enquiries at a lower cost per lead |
| Main risk | Wasting budget on the wrong clicks | Waiting months for work that was done badly |

## How Google Ads works, in plain terms

You choose the searches you want to appear for (keywords such as "CA firm in Surat" or "laser hair removal Adajan"), write an ad, and set an average daily budget. When someone searches, Google runs an auction and decides which ads to show. For most search campaigns you pay only when someone clicks.

A few things business owners are often surprised by:

- **Your daily budget is an average, not a hard cap.** Google says that on any single day it may spend up to **2 times** your average daily budget, but over a month you will not be charged more than about **30.4 times** that daily average. See [About spending limits](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10486637) in Google Ads Help.
- **Clicks are not customers.** A click is a visit. If your landing page is slow, unclear or has no easy way to call or WhatsApp, you pay for the visit and get nothing back.
- **Taxes are extra.** In India, applicable taxes such as GST are charged on top of your ad spend. Google lists the details for each country on its [Taxes in your country](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375370) page.
- **Competition sets the price.** In crowded categories, clicks cost more because more businesses are bidding for the same searches.

## How SEO works, in plain terms

SEO (search engine optimisation) means making your website and your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) the best match for what people search. That includes a separate page for each service, clear mentions of your area, fast loading on mobile, genuine reviews, and useful content that answers the questions buyers ask.

Google does not charge for these normal listings. What you invest is time, either your own or an agency's. The catch is patience: a new or weak website usually needs a few months before it ranks well for searches that bring real enquiries, and competitive searches take longer.

SEO also feeds newer places people look for businesses. AI answers in Google and tools like ChatGPT draw on websites that clearly explain what a business does and where. We cover this in [ChatGPT vs Google in 2026](/insights/chatgpt-vs-google-in-2026-how-businesses-get-found).

## When Google Ads makes more sense

- **You need enquiries this month**, for example a new clinic, showroom or office that has just opened.
- **You are testing a new service or product** and want to know quickly whether people search for it and enquire.
- **Your offer is seasonal or time-bound**, like a festive sale, an admission season or tax filing deadlines.
- **Your website is new** and will not rank on its own for a while.
- **Each customer is worth a lot**, so even a higher cost per enquiry still pays off.

## When SEO makes more sense

- **You want a steady flow of enquiries** without paying for every visit for years.
- **Your budget is limited**, and a monthly ad spend would be hard to sustain.
- **People search for your service locally**, such as "dentist near me" or "interior designer in Vesu". Map results and normal results get attention that ads cannot buy.
- **Buyers research before they decide.** B2B buyers, people choosing a doctor or a CA, and anyone comparing options will often read several websites first.
- **You plan to be in business for years.** Every good page you add keeps working long after it is written.

## Why most businesses should use both

Ads and SEO help each other when they are run together:

1. **Ads show you which searches bring paying customers.** That tells you which service pages to build and improve for SEO.
2. **SEO lowers what you need to spend on ads** over time, because more enquiries come from free listings.
3. **The same website does both jobs.** A fast page with a clear offer, prices or price ranges where possible, and a visible call and WhatsApp button improves ad results and rankings alike.

A sensible pattern for many small businesses: run a small, tightly focused ad campaign on your most profitable service while the website and Google Business Profile are being built up, then shift budget towards SEO and content as organic enquiries grow.

## Mistakes that waste money on either one

- **Sending ads to the homepage** instead of a page about the exact service the person searched for
- **Not tracking calls, WhatsApp clicks and form submissions**, so you cannot tell which keywords bring enquiries
- **Broad keywords with no negative keywords**, so you pay for searches like "free", "jobs" or "course" when you sell a service
- **Ignoring your Google Business Profile**, which often brings more local calls than the website itself
- **Buying cheap SEO packages** that promise "page one in 30 days" or sell bulk backlinks. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) warn against link schemes, and the damage can take a long time to fix
- **Judging SEO after four weeks**, or judging ads without enough clicks to learn anything

## How to decide in five questions

1. **Do I need enquiries within the next 30 days?** If yes, start with ads.
2. **Can I keep paying for ads every month for a year?** If not, invest in SEO early.
3. **Is my website ready to convert visitors?** If not, fix that first. Neither ads nor SEO can save a page that does not make it easy to contact you.
4. **Do people in my city search for what I sell?** If yes, local SEO and Google Maps should be part of the plan.
5. **Do I know my most profitable service?** Put ad money and SEO effort there first.

## FAQ

**Is SEO free?** Showing up in normal Google results costs nothing per click, but the work to get there is not free. It takes time, writing and technical effort, whether you do it yourself or hire someone.

**How much should a small business spend on Google Ads?** There is no fixed minimum. Start with an amount you can sustain for at least a month or two so you collect enough data, focus on one or two services, and judge results by the cost per enquiry, not the number of clicks.

**Does running Google Ads improve my SEO rankings?** No. Paying for ads does not change where Google ranks your normal listings. The two are separate, although data from ads can guide your SEO decisions.

**How long does SEO take to show results?** Usually a few months for a new or weak website, and longer in competitive industries. Local searches with a well-optimised Google Business Profile can move faster.

**Should I try Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) instead?** They work differently. Google Ads reaches people who are already searching for what you sell. Meta ads reach people based on interests and location, which suits products and offers people did not know they wanted yet. Many businesses use both.
