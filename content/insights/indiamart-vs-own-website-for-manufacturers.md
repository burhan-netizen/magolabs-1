For most manufacturers in Surat and Gujarat, the answer is **both, with different jobs**. B2B marketplaces like IndiaMART can bring a steady stream of enquiries quickly, but you share every page with competitors and the buyer is often comparing prices. **Your own website** builds trust, wins the serious buyers who check you before they call, and keeps working for you without a listing fee or competing ads beside your name.

The mistake we see most often is treating a marketplace listing as a replacement for a website. Here's how the two compare, and how to use them together.

## The quick comparison

| | B2B marketplace (IndiaMART and similar) | Your own website |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed to get enquiries | Fast, once your listing is live | Slower, builds over months |
| Who you compete with | Competitors listed on the same page | Nobody on your own pages |
| Lead quality | Mixed: many price-only and bulk enquiries | Usually fewer, but more serious buyers |
| Control over presentation | Limited to the platform's format | Full control: story, photos, process, proof |
| Trust | Platform badges help, but you look like one of many | Builds your own brand and credibility |
| Ongoing cost | Subscription or paid packages to stay visible | Hosting and upkeep, no per-lead charges |
| If you stop paying | Visibility drops | Website stays, rankings take time to fade |
| Ownership | The platform owns the page and the buyer journey | You own the domain, content and data |

## Why manufacturers like marketplaces

- **Buyers already search there.** Many procurement teams and traders start on B2B portals.
- **Enquiries arrive quickly**, which matters when you're starting out or entering new markets.
- **No technical work.** You fill in a listing and upload photos.

For a new manufacturer or a commodity product, a marketplace can be a sensible first step.

## Where marketplaces fall short

- **You're one of many.** A buyer sees your product next to competitors, often sorted by price. That pulls every conversation toward discounts.
- **Lead quality varies.** A common complaint is enquiries for tiny quantities, products outside your category, or from people simply collecting quotes.
- **Your brand disappears.** Buyers remember "the supplier from IndiaMART", not your company name.
- **You can't tell your full story:** your plant, capacity, quality process, certifications and the clients you supply.
- **Visibility depends on paying.** If you reduce your package, you may drop down the listings.

## What your own website does that a listing can't

### Builds trust with serious buyers

Before a large order, buyers want to know you're real and capable. A professional website with your facility, machinery, process, quality checks, certifications and contact details answers that in minutes. Many buyers who find you on a marketplace will search your company name next. What they find decides whether they call.

### Ranks for specific searches

A page for each product line, with the product name and location, can appear on Google for searches like "PP woven fabric manufacturer Gujarat" or "industrial valve manufacturer Surat". These searchers are looking for a manufacturer, not comparing ten listings.

### Supports exports

International buyers rarely rely on Indian marketplaces alone. They look for a credible company website, ideally with technical specifications, capacity and export experience.

### Gives you a link to send

Instead of forwarding fifty photos on WhatsApp, you send one link that has everything: products, specifications, capacity and contact.

## How to use both together

1. **Keep your marketplace listing if it brings real orders**, but treat it as one lead source, not your online presence.
2. **Put your website address on your listing**, catalogues, visiting cards, invoices and email signature.
3. **Make sure your company name search shows your website**, Google Business Profile and listing, all with matching details.
4. **Build a page for each product line** on your website with specifications, applications, MOQ and an enquiry button.
5. **Track where orders come from.** Ask every new buyer how they found you. After a few months you'll know what each channel is worth.

## What a manufacturer's website should include

- What you make and for which industries, in the first screen
- A page for each product line with specifications, applications and MOQ
- Your plant, machinery and capacity, with real photos
- Quality process, testing and certifications
- Industries or types of clients you supply (check permissions before naming clients)
- Export capability, if relevant
- Clear contact options: phone, WhatsApp, email, enquiry form and location

## FAQ

**Is IndiaMART enough for a small manufacturer?** It can bring early enquiries, but without your own website you have no presence you control, and serious buyers may doubt you.

**Will a website bring leads immediately?** Usually not. A new website takes time to rank on Google. Marketplaces are faster at first; a website compounds over time.

**Should my website and listing have the same content?** Use consistent business details, but write more on your website: your process, capacity and specifications. Avoid copying the same text everywhere.

**Do I need a full ecommerce site?** Most B2B manufacturers don't. A catalogue with enquiry buttons fits the way trade buyers actually order.
