A real estate builder's website needs to do two things well: give home buyers enough clear, honest information to book a site visit, and follow RERA's advertising rules for every project it shows. In practice that means a separate page for each project with real floor plans, specifications, location, amenities and construction progress, a quick way to call or WhatsApp the sales team, and the project's **RERA registration number, the GujRERA website address and the project QR code** shown clearly. Under the RERA Act, your website counts as advertising, so it is held to the same rules as your hoardings and newspaper ads.

Builders and developers in Surat, Ahmedabad and across Gujarat often spend heavily on brochures, hoardings and portal listings, then treat the website as an afterthought. Buyers do the opposite: they look you up online before they ever visit the site office. Here is what a builder website should include, and what the law expects from it.

## Why your website counts as an advertisement under RERA

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 defines an "advertisement" widely. It covers any notice, brochure, leaflet or publication offering a project for sale, in any medium, and that includes the internet. So the project pages on your website, your Instagram posts, your Google ads and the PDF brochure you send on WhatsApp are all advertisements in the eyes of the law.

Two sections matter most:

- **Section 3:** a project that needs registration cannot be advertised, marketed, booked or sold before it is registered with the state RERA authority. Do not put a "coming soon, book now" page for an unregistered project on your website.
- **Section 11(2):** every advertisement or prospectus for a registered project must prominently mention the RERA authority's website address and the project's registration number, along with other details as required.

Section 61 of the Act allows a penalty of up to 5% of the estimated project cost for contraventions, so this is not a detail to leave to the designer's taste.

## What GujRERA asks for in Gujarat

GujRERA has added its own requirements on top of the Act through circulars:

1. **Project QR code:** since 1 October 2024, advertisements, brochures and prospectuses must include the project's QR code from its RERA registration certificate, so a buyer can scan it and open the project's details on the GujRERA portal.
2. **A standard RERA details block:** newer guidelines, effective from 15 June 2025, require the QR code, registration number and GujRERA website address to appear together in a prominent block, no smaller than the builder's own contact details, with set placement for print, multi-page brochures, social media posts, videos and audio ads.
3. **Booking forms and letters:** the same details are expected on booking forms and communication to allottees, not only on ads.

GujRERA publishes its circulars and templates on the [GujRERA portal](https://gujrera.gujarat.gov.in/). Rules get updated, so check the latest circular, or ask your RERA consultant, before a project launch. If you build outside Gujarat, your state's authority (MahaRERA, K-RERA and others) has its own version of these rules.

### How to show RERA details properly on a website

- Put the RERA details block **near the top of each project page**, not hidden in the footer in tiny grey text.
- Use a **separate block for each project**. One registration number on the home page does not cover every project.
- Show the **QR code as a sharp image**, large enough to scan from a phone screen held by someone else, and link the registration number to the project's page on the GujRERA portal.
- Add the same block to **every downloadable brochure** and to the first frame or slide of your project videos and social posts.
- Keep **phase-wise registrations** clear. If Tower A and Tower B are registered separately, show both numbers against the right towers.
- **Match the website to the registered details.** Carpet area, number of units, amenities and possession date on the site should agree with what you filed with RERA.

## What home buyers look for on a builder website

Compliance keeps you out of trouble. The content below is what actually gets buyers to call.

| Section | What to include | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project page | Configuration (2 BHK, 3 BHK, shops, offices), carpet area, tower and floor count | Buyers shortlist by size and type first |
| Floor plans | Clear plans for each unit type, labelled in carpet area | The most viewed part of any project page |
| Location | Embedded Google Map, distances to schools, hospitals, highways and the station | Location decides most purchases |
| Specifications | Flooring, fittings, lifts, parking, power backup, water | Answers questions your sales team hears daily |
| Amenities | Real photos once built, renders clearly labelled as artist's impressions | Builds trust and avoids misleading claims |
| Construction progress | Dated photos or videos, updated every month or two | Shows the project is moving and you are reliable |
| Completed projects | Past projects with photos and possession years | Your track record is your strongest selling point |
| Approvals and documents | RERA details, and other approvals you are comfortable sharing | Serious buyers and their banks check these |
| Contact | Click to call, WhatsApp, site visit form and site office address | Turns interest into a visit |

A few points make a big difference:

- **Carpet area, not only super built-up area.** RERA defines carpet area and expects it to be stated. Buyers compare projects on it.
- **Label renders honestly.** Use "artist's impression" on renders and avoid promises your agreement does not back, such as "guaranteed returns" or amenities that are not in the approved plan.
- **Keep sold-out and completed projects up.** Move them to a "completed projects" section instead of deleting them. They prove you deliver.
- **Make the page fast on mobile.** Most buyers open your link from a WhatsApp message or an Instagram ad on their phone. Heavy 3D walkthroughs and huge brochures should load only when tapped.

## Common mistakes on builder websites

- Advertising a new project with "pre-launch offers" before it has a RERA registration.
- One RERA number in the footer for a site that lists five projects.
- A blurry QR code that does not scan, or one copied from the wrong project.
- Old possession dates that no longer match the project's registered or extended timeline.
- Only renders and no real progress photos, which makes buyers doubt the project.
- PDF brochures with no RERA block, shared on WhatsApp thousands of times.
- A slow, image-heavy site that never loads on a buyer's phone.

## FAQ

**Is a builder's website an advertisement under RERA?** Yes. The Act's definition of advertisement covers publications in any medium, including the internet, so project pages must follow the same rules as print ads.

**Do I need the RERA number on my company home page?** Show the registration details on each project's own page and wherever a project is promoted. A home page that features a project should carry that project's details too.

**Can I show a project on my website before RERA registration?** Not if the project needs registration. Section 3 bars advertising, marketing and booking before registration. Small projects exempt under the Act are an exception, so check with your consultant.

**Where do I get the QR code?** It is on the project's RERA registration certificate issued by GujRERA. Use that exact code.

**Does this apply to Instagram and Facebook posts?** Yes. GujRERA's guidelines cover social media posts and videos as well, with set placement for the RERA details.
