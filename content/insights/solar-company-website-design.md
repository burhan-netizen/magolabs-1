A solar company website has to convince a careful buyer that **you can deliver at scale**. Corporate and industrial buyers research suppliers before they sign. They want to see your capabilities, the products and systems you handle, the kind of projects you take on, and a direct way to start a conversation.

**SolWay Energies** is a solar EPC and distribution business working with corporate buyers. When they came to us, they had **no website** to back up the conversations happening in their meetings and on calls. We built one designed to show scale and capability. Here is what it needed, and what any solar or energy business can learn from it.

## The problem: strong sales calls, nothing to find afterwards

In B2B energy, a deal rarely closes on the first call. After a good meeting, the buyer goes away and does their homework. They search the company name, share it with a colleague, and look for proof the supplier is real and capable.

With no website, every sales conversation SolWay had was quietly losing credibility afterwards. The buyer had nothing to check and nothing to forward to the people who sign off on the purchase.

## What a solar company website needs

### 1. Capability and scale, stated clearly

Corporate buyers need to know you can handle their size of project. The site should make clear what you do, residential, commercial or industrial, and whether you handle EPC, distribution or both. We structured the SolWay site around the **proof a B2B buyer looks for** before trusting a new supplier.

### 2. A clear showcase of products and services

Buyers compare panels, inverters, systems and service scope. A clear **product and service showcase** lets them check fit quickly, without needing a call just to find out what you offer. It also gives Google pages to rank for the specific solutions people search for.

### 3. Something worth forwarding

In companies, the person you meet is rarely the only decision maker. Your website is what they forward to finance, operations and management. It should explain your offer clearly to someone who was not in the meeting.

### 4. Lead capture on every page

When a buyer is ready, the next step should be obvious. The SolWay site uses **direct lead capture** throughout, so enquiries come straight to the team with the details needed to follow up properly.

### 5. A strong, modern look

Solar is a modern, technology-led industry. A dated or cluttered website works against that. Strong imagery, clean layouts and confident copy make the business look as current as the systems it installs.

## The result

SolWay Energies now has a digital presence that **supports its sales conversations instead of undermining them**, giving corporate buyers a reason to take the relationship seriously from the first search. ## A quick checklist for your solar company website

- Does it state clearly who you serve and what you deliver?
- Can a buyer see your products and services without calling?
- Would it make sense to a decision maker who never met you?
- Is there a clear way to send an enquiry on every page?
- Does it look as modern as the technology you sell?

## Common mistakes on solar company websites

**Only talking about the planet.** Sustainability matters, but a corporate buyer is deciding on cost, reliability and delivery. Lead with what they need to know.

**No clarity on who you serve.** Residential, commercial and industrial buyers have different needs. A site that does not say which you focus on loses all three.

**Generic product information.** Copying manufacturer brochures adds nothing. Explain the systems you supply and install, and why buyers choose them.

**No proof of delivery.** Buyers want to know you have done this before. Describe the kinds of projects you take on and how you handle them.

**Weak lead capture.** If the only way to enquire is a contact page, ready buyers slip away. Make the next step obvious on every page.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a solar EPC company website include?

Who you serve, the services you deliver (design, supply, installation and maintenance), the products and systems you work with, the kinds of projects you take on, and a clear way to request a quote or site assessment.

### How does a website help B2B solar sales?

Most corporate buyers research you after the first meeting and share your website with colleagues who sign off on the purchase. A clear site supports your sales team in conversations they are not part of.

### Should a solar company show prices on its website?

For commercial and industrial projects, usually not, since every site is different. Explain what affects the cost and invite buyers to request an assessment instead.

### How do solar companies get more leads online?

A clear website, local SEO for the areas you serve, a complete Google Business Profile and a simple quote request on every page are the foundation.
