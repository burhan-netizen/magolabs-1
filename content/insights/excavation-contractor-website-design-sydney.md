A website for an excavation or civil contractor has to do what a site visit does: **show builders the size and quality of your work, and make it easy to ask for a quote**. Builders and developers check a contractor online before they trust them with a site. Real job photos, a clear list of services, the builders you already work with and a fast quote form do most of the convincing.

**Jaymo Civil** is a Sydney excavation and civil contractor trusted by builders for bulk and detail excavation, shoring, remediation works and service trenches. When they came to us, their **old website had gone offline**. A builder checking them out found nothing at all. Here is how we rebuilt it, and what any civil or earthmoving business can take from it.

## The problem: a serious crew that looked small online

Excavation work is won on reputation. A builder hears your name from another builder, then looks you up. If the search turns up a dead link or nothing, the crew looks smaller and less organised than it really is, and the builder calls the next name on the list.

For a contractor taking on large jobs, that gap between real capability and online presence costs work you never hear about.

## What an excavation contractor website needs

### 1. Real jobs, not stock photos

Builders can spot a stock photo of a digger instantly. What convinces them is your own work: real sites, real machines, real results. For Jaymo, we built a **3D gallery of real projects from across Sydney**, so the first thing a builder sees is proof of the jobs the crew takes on.

### 2. A clear section for each service

Bulk excavation, detail excavation, shoring, remediation and service trenches are different jobs, searched for by different people. Each service got its own clear section, so a builder can confirm in seconds that Jaymo does exactly what they need. Separate service content also helps Google match the business to those specific searches.

### 3. Social proof that builders recognise

Nothing reassures a builder like seeing names they know. We added a **wall of the builders Jaymo works with**. It says more in one glance than a paragraph of claims ever could.

### 4. One next step: get a quote

Every section of the site points to the same action: **request a quote or call**. The quote form lands straight in Jaymo's inbox, so no enquiry sits unread. On a phone, the call button is always one tap away, because many builders are on site when they look you up.

### 5. A brand that feels as solid as the work

We designed a premium, corporate site in Jaymo's own **black and yellow**, opening with their logo animation. Heavy civil work deserves a site that feels strong and dependable, not a template that could belong to any trade.

### 6. Back on Google

A website is only half of being found. We also set up and optimised Jaymo's **Google Business Profile**, so the business shows up on Google and Maps again when builders search for excavation contractors in Sydney.

## The result

Jaymo Civil now has a website that matches the size of the jobs they take on. Builders see real work, real clients and a clear way to get a quote, and the business shows up on Google again. In the owner's words, the team "smashed it".

## A quick checklist for your contractor website

- Is your site live, fast and working on a phone?
- Does it show real photos of your own jobs?
- Is each service explained on its own?
- Can a builder see who you already work with?
- Is a quote request one tap away from every page?
- Is your Google Business Profile set up and active?
