A CA firm should redesign its website when the site **no longer reflects how the firm works**: it looks dated, breaks on a phone, or brings in no enquiries. For an accountancy practice, an old website can do more harm than having none, because it signals the opposite of what clients want from their accountant: care, precision and being up to date.

**Jay Mehta & Co.**, a chartered accountancy practice, had exactly this problem. Its existing website looked unprofessional and was not turning visitors into enquiries. We revamped the whole site from the ground up. Here is what changed, and how to tell if your firm needs the same.

## The problem: a website that worked against the firm

A prospective client judges an accountant on detail. If the website has broken layouts, old design, small unreadable text on a phone and no obvious way to get in touch, the visitor quietly assumes the firm's work might be the same.

That is why an outdated site can be worse than no site. With no website, a client simply knows nothing. With a poor one, they form the wrong impression.

## What the redesign fixed

### 1. A clean, trust-focused layout

We replaced the old design with a **clean, trust-focused layout** built to convert. Clear headings, generous space, real photos of the CA and the practice, and services laid out in the order a client thinks about them.

### 2. Built properly for mobile

Most visitors arrive on a phone. The new site was **optimised for mobile** from the start: fast to load, easy to read without zooming, and with call and enquiry buttons in reach of a thumb.

### 3. A clearer enquiry form

The old site made getting in touch harder than it should be. We built a **clearer enquiry form**, short and simple, alongside call and WhatsApp options, so the step from interest to enquiry is easy.

### 4. A site that matches how the firm actually works

The goal was a website that finally matched how the firm operates day to day. Jay Mehta & Co. serves clients across India and abroad, so the site needed to feel as professional as the service those clients already receive.

### 5. Within ICAI guidelines

A redesign is a good moment to check your content against the rules. See our guide to [ICAI website guidelines for CA firms](/insights/icai-website-guidelines-ca-firm-website).

## The result

Jay Mehta & Co. now has a website that works smoothly on mobile, loads fast, and gives clients a much stronger reason to trust the practice than the old site ever did. In the firm's own words, they finally have a site that is **clear, professional and easy to trust**, and the project ran from structure to launch without them having to chase for updates.

## Signs your CA firm website needs a redesign

- It looks noticeably older than other firms' sites in your city.
- Text is hard to read or buttons are hard to tap on a phone.
- It has not brought in an enquiry in months.
- You would hesitate to send the link to a new client.
- Your services or team have changed, but the site has not.

For a fuller list, read [12 signs your business website needs a redesign](/insights/signs-your-business-website-needs-a-redesign).

## Common mistakes when redesigning a CA website

**Copying the old content across.** A new design with the same unclear text fixes little. Use the redesign to rewrite services in the words clients search for.

**Choosing a template.** Templates make your firm look like dozens of others. A firm built on trust deserves a site designed around its own clients.

**Losing what already ranks.** If parts of the old site appear on Google, a redesign should keep or properly redirect those pages so you do not lose that visibility.

**Forgetting mobile.** Many redesigns still look great on a desktop and awkward on a phone. Test on a real phone before launch.

**Adding features nobody uses.** Chatbots, sliders and pop-ups often slow the site and distract from the one thing that matters: getting in touch.
