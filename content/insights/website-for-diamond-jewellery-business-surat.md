A website for a diamond or jewellery business in Surat has to do one thing above all: **build trust before the first conversation**. That means sharp, accurate product photography, clear details on **certification, sourcing and quality processes**, who you supply, and a simple way to enquire privately by phone, WhatsApp or email. For retail jewellery, add pricing approach, hallmarking information and how to visit or order.

Surat cuts and polishes a large share of the world's diamonds, and buyers in Mumbai, Antwerp, Dubai, Hong Kong and New York increasingly check a supplier online before they deal. A dated website, or none at all, makes even an established firm look small. Here's what a strong one includes.

## Know which buyer you're speaking to

| Business type | Main visitors | What they need to see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diamond manufacturer | Traders, jewellery manufacturers, international buyers | Capabilities, goods range, certification, capacity, compliance |
| Diamond trader | Retailers, jewellers, wholesalers | Inventory types, sizes and qualities, certification, terms |
| Lab-grown diamond business | Jewellery brands, retailers, exporters | Growing or sourcing method, grading, disclosure, range |
| Jewellery manufacturer | Retail jewellers, brands, exporters | Collections, craftsmanship, MOQ, customisation, process |
| Retail jeweller | Local customers, wedding buyers | Collections, hallmarking, pricing approach, showroom visit |

Trying to serve all of these on one homepage confuses everyone. Lead with your main buyer and give others their own pages.

## What the website needs

### 1. A clear, premium first impression

Diamonds and jewellery are about trust and taste. The design should feel calm and premium: generous space, restrained colours, excellent photography. A cluttered page with flashing offers undermines the product.

### 2. Accurate product presentation

- High-resolution photos and, where possible, short videos showing sparkle and detail
- Consistent backgrounds and lighting across products
- Specifications in a clear format: shape, carat ranges, colour and clarity ranges, cut, certification lab
- For jewellery: metal, purity, weights, stone details and customisation options

Photos must match the real product. Over-edited images damage trust the moment a buyer sees the goods.

### 3. Certification and disclosure

Buyers want to know how quality is verified. Explain which grading laboratories your stones are certified by, how natural and lab-grown diamonds are clearly disclosed, and how you handle sourcing and compliance. For jewellery sold in India, explain hallmarking: BIS hallmarking is mandatory for gold jewellery in notified districts (see the hallmarking section of the [Bureau of Indian Standards](https://www.bis.gov.in/) website for the current list of districts).

### 4. Company credibility

Years in business, your facility and team, memberships of trade bodies, exhibitions you attend, and markets you export to. Real photos of your office and workshop matter far more than stock images.

### 5. Private, easy enquiries

High-value buyers prefer discreet contact. Offer a direct call option, WhatsApp, and a short enquiry form. For trade buyers, make it easy to request a stock list or a meeting. Respond quickly: speed signals professionalism.

### 6. International readiness

If you export, write in clear international English, mention the markets you serve, show time zones or availability, and make sure the site loads fast outside India too.

## Should you sell jewellery online?

For retail jewellers, an online catalogue with "enquire" or "book a showroom visit" buttons is often the right first step. Full ecommerce for high-value jewellery needs secure payments, insured shipping, returns policies and strong trust signals, so plan it carefully. Many jewellers use the website to bring customers into the showroom rather than to complete the sale online.

## Getting found on Google

- A page for each product type and collection, with clear titles
- A complete Google Business Profile for your office or showroom
- Content that answers buyer questions: certification, lab-grown vs natural, buying guides
- Consistent business details across your website, profile and trade directories

## Common mistakes

- A homepage with a slow, auto-playing video and no information
- Stock photos of diamonds that aren't yours
- No mention of certification or disclosure
- Contact details hidden or only a generic form
- An outdated site that hasn't changed in years, which makes buyers wonder if the business is still active

## FAQ

**Do diamond traders need a website if they sell through brokers?** A website gives brokers and buyers something credible to check and share, which supports deals you'd make anyway.

**Should I show prices?** Trade prices usually depend on goods and quantity, so most businesses show ranges or "price on request". Retail jewellers can show prices or price ranges for collections.

**Should natural and lab-grown diamonds be on the same website?** If you sell both, disclose clearly and keep them in separate, clearly labelled sections.

**How important is photography?** Critical. For diamonds and jewellery, photography quality directly affects how buyers judge your goods.
