A simple, professional business website can be live in **about a week**. A website built to generate enquiries, with several service pages and proper SEO, usually takes **2 to 3 weeks**. An online store takes **around 4 weeks**. At Mago Labs, our four packages launch in **5, 14, 21 and 28 days**, depending on size.

Those numbers assume one thing that most timelines quietly ignore: **the content is ready on time**. In our experience, the build itself is rarely what slows a website down. Waiting for photos, text, logins and feedback is. Here is how the time actually breaks down, and how to keep your project on schedule.

## Typical timelines by type of website

| Type of website | Typical timeline | What it usually includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple business website | 5 to 7 days | A few pages, contact details, WhatsApp and call buttons |
| Lead-generating website | 2 to 3 weeks | Separate service pages, enquiry forms, on-page SEO |
| Premium or larger website | About 3 weeks | More pages, custom design details, richer content |
| Ecommerce website | About 4 weeks | Product catalogue, cart, payments, shipping setup |
| Complex custom platform | 1 to 3 months or more | Logins, dashboards, integrations, custom features |

These are working timelines for a focused team. A freelancer juggling many clients, or an agency with a long queue, may quote longer for the same site.

## The stages of building a website

### 1. Discovery and planning (1 to 3 days)

Before any design, the agency needs to understand your business: what you sell, who buys it, which pages you need, and what a visitor should do (call, WhatsApp, fill a form, buy). A good plan here saves days of rework later.

**What you provide:** a short brief, examples of websites you like, and your competitors' names.

### 2. Content collection (often the longest stage)

Every page needs words and images. That means your services or products, prices if you show them, photos of your work, team or premises, client logos, testimonials and your logo files.

**Why this causes delays:** content depends on the business owner, who is also running the business. A website that could launch in two weeks often waits a month for photos. If writing is not your strength, ask whether the agency writes the copy for you. It is usually faster.

### 3. Design (2 to 7 days)

The designer lays out the homepage and key pages. You review and ask for changes. On a well-planned project, one or two rounds of feedback is normal.

**What slows it down:** feedback spread across weeks, or several decision-makers who each want different changes. Agree internally on who gives final approval before design starts.

### 4. Development (3 to 14 days)

The approved design is built into a working website: fast loading, mobile friendly, forms connected, WhatsApp and call buttons working, and pages set up for Google. An online store adds products, payment gateway setup and shipping rules, which is why it takes longer.

### 5. Review, testing and launch (1 to 3 days)

You check the site on your phone and computer, the agency fixes final details, then connects your domain and puts the site live. It should also be submitted to Google Search Console so Google can find it.

## What makes a website take longer

- **Content arriving late.** The biggest cause by far.
- **No clear decision-maker.** Feedback from several people that contradicts itself.
- **Changing the scope mid-way.** Adding a store or a booking system halfway through resets part of the plan.
- **Domain and hosting access.** Not knowing who registered your domain, or losing the login, can hold up launch for days.
- **Payment gateway approval.** For online stores in India, gateway verification (KYC and document checks) runs on the payment company's timeline, not the agency's.
- **Custom features.** Anything connecting to other software, such as a CRM, ERP or inventory system, adds time.

## How to get your website live faster

1. **Gather content before you start.** Logo, photos, a list of services and contact details. Even rough notes help.
2. **Pick one person to approve.** One clear voice keeps feedback fast and consistent.
3. **Give feedback in one go.** A single list of changes is quicker than ten separate messages.
4. **Find your domain login early.** Check who registered it and that you can access it.
5. **Launch, then grow.** Start with the pages that matter most and add a blog or extra pages after launch.

## Does a faster website mean a worse website?

Not necessarily. Speed comes from a clear process, not cut corners. A small business site does not need two months. But be wary of anyone promising a full custom ecommerce store in two days, or a "website in an hour" that is really a template with your name on it. Ask what is included, who writes the content, and whether the site is built for mobile and for Google.

## When does a new website start showing on Google?

Launch day is not the day you start getting traffic from Google. A new website usually takes **a few weeks to a few months** to start ranking for searches, depending on your industry and competition. Submitting the site to Google Search Console, setting up your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) and building proper service pages all help speed this up.

## FAQ

**Can a website be built in one day?** A basic one-page site can be, but a proper business website with several pages, real content and SEO setup takes at least several days to do well.

**How long does a WordPress website take compared with a custom website?** The platform matters less than the scope and the content. A simple site takes about a week either way; the planning, content and feedback stages take the same time on any platform.

**How long does a website redesign take?** Usually similar to a new site of the same size. Existing content can speed it up, but only if it is still accurate and worth keeping.

**Who should write the content for my website?** You know your business best, but many owners find it faster to share notes or answer questions and let the agency write the final copy.
