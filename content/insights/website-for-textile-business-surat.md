A good website for a textile business in Surat does three jobs: it **shows buyers your range clearly** (fabrics, sarees, dress materials or yarn, with real photos and specifications), it **answers trade questions before they ask** (MOQ, pricing structure, dispatch, payment terms), and it **turns interest into WhatsApp or phone enquiries** in one tap. It also needs to be found on Google when buyers search for what you make.

Surat's textile trade has always run on relationships, market visits and WhatsApp. That still works. But more buyers, from other states and abroad, now check a supplier online before they call. If they can't find you, or find a site that looks abandoned, they move to the next name. Here's what your website needs.

## Who is visiting a textile website?

Before planning pages, be clear who you're selling to:

- **Wholesalers and traders** in other cities looking for reliable supply
- **Boutiques and retailers** buying in smaller quantities
- **Brands and exporters** looking for manufacturers or job work
- **Garment manufacturers** sourcing fabric
- **Retail customers**, if you also sell directly

Each wants different information. A boutique owner wants to see designs and minimum quantities. An exporter wants capacity, certifications and quality processes. Most Surat textile sites try to speak to everyone and end up speaking to no one.

## The pages a textile business website needs

### 1. A homepage that says what you make, and for whom

In the first screen: what you manufacture or trade, who you supply, where you're based, and a WhatsApp button. "Manufacturers of printed georgette and chiffon fabrics for wholesalers and boutiques, Surat" beats "Welcome to our company".

### 2. Product or catalogue pages by category

Group products the way buyers search: by fabric type, product type or occasion. For each category show:

- Clear photos in good light, including close-ups of fabric texture and print
- Fabric, width, GSM or weight, colours and variants
- Minimum order quantity
- A WhatsApp button that sends the product name with the enquiry

You don't need to show prices if you trade on quotes, but say how pricing works ("prices on request, depend on quantity").

### 3. A "how we work" page for trade buyers

MOQs, how to place an order, sampling, dispatch times, payment terms, transport partners and whether you ship across India or export. These are the questions buyers ask on every call. Answering them on the site saves your team time and filters serious buyers.

### 4. Manufacturing and quality

For manufacturers: machinery, capacity, processes (weaving, dyeing, printing, embroidery), quality checks and any certifications. Real photos of your unit build far more trust than stock images.

### 5. About and contact

Your story, years in business, your market location if you have a shop or office in a textile market, a map, phone, WhatsApp and email. Business details like GSTIN help buyers trust you.

## Should a Surat textile business sell online?

It depends on who you sell to:

| You mainly sell to | Best website type |
| --- | --- |
| Wholesalers and traders (B2B) | Catalogue website with WhatsApp enquiries |
| Boutiques in smaller quantities | Catalogue with MOQ details, optionally online orders for sets |
| Retail customers across India | Full ecommerce store with payments and shipping |
| Brands and exporters | Company website focused on capacity, quality and process |

Many textile businesses do best with a **catalogue website plus WhatsApp**, then add ecommerce later for retail. Building a full online store before you're ready to handle retail orders and returns creates work without sales.

## How textile buyers find suppliers on Google

Buyers search for specific things: "georgette fabric manufacturer Surat", "saree wholesaler Surat", "embroidery job work Surat", "rayon fabric supplier Gujarat". To show up:

- **One page per product category**, with the product and "Surat" in the page title and heading
- **A complete Google Business Profile** with your market or unit location, categories and photos (see [why a business doesn't show on Google Maps](/insights/why-is-my-business-not-showing-on-google-maps))
- **Descriptive image names and text**, because Google can't read a photo of a saree; it reads the words around it
- **Fast loading on mobile data**, since heavy, uncompressed product photos are the most common problem on textile websites

## Common mistakes on textile websites

- A homepage slider of models with no information about what you make
- Hundreds of product photos with no names, categories or details
- No WhatsApp button, or one that only opens a blank chat
- PDF catalogues as the only way to see products (slow on phones and invisible to Google)
- Outdated products and old phone numbers
- No mention of MOQ, so every enquiry starts with the same question

## FAQ

**How many products should I put on my website?** Start with your best-selling categories and your strongest designs. Twenty well-photographed products with details beat two hundred blurry ones.

**Do I need a website if I sell through WhatsApp groups?** WhatsApp works for existing buyers. A website helps new buyers find and trust you, and gives you a link to send instead of fifty photos.

**Can I show prices?** If your prices depend on quantity, say "price on request" and explain how pricing works. If you sell retail, show prices.
