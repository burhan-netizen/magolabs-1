Most small business websites need **five core pages**: a **Home** page, a **Services or Products** page (ideally one page per main service), an **About** page, a **Contact** page, and a **Privacy Policy**. Depending on your business, you then add pages for **your work or projects**, **pricing**, **FAQs**, **locations** or a **blog**. A plumber in Surat might need six pages in total. A manufacturer with twenty product lines might need forty. The right number is the number of separate things your customers search for and ask about.

The mistake we see most often is not having too few pages. It is putting everything on one long homepage, so Google has nothing specific to show for "kitchen cabinet maker in Adajan" and the visitor has to scroll to find the one thing they came for. Here is what each page is for and what to put on it.

## The core pages every business website needs

| Page | Its job | Must include |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Tell visitors in five seconds what you do, where, and how to contact you | One clear headline, your main services, a call and WhatsApp button, a reason to trust you |
| Services or Products | Answer "do they do the exact thing I need?" | One section or page per service, who it is for, what is included, next step |
| About | Show there are real, experienced people behind the business | Your story, years in business, team or founder photo, address |
| Contact | Make getting in touch effortless | Phone, WhatsApp, email, address, map, hours, a short form |
| Privacy Policy | Explain what you do with the details people submit | What you collect, why, who sees it, how to ask for deletion |

### Home page

Your homepage is a signpost, not an encyclopedia. Within the first screen on a phone, a visitor should know what you do, which area you serve and how to reach you. Below that, link to each main service, show proof (photos of real work, client names you are allowed to share, years in business) and repeat the contact options.

**Skip:** sliders that rotate five messages, a "Welcome to our website" headline, and long paragraphs about your vision.

### Service pages (one per main service)

This is the page type most small business sites get wrong, and the one that matters most for Google. If you offer interior design, modular kitchens and office fit-outs, give each its own page. Each page can then target the words people actually search, answer that service's specific questions, and show photos of that kind of work.

A good service page covers:

- What the service is, in plain words, and who it is for
- What is included, and what is not
- How the process works, step by step
- Photos or examples of that specific work
- Common questions about that service
- A clear next step: call, WhatsApp or a short form

If you sell products, the same idea applies: a page per product category, and a page per product if customers search for products by name or model.

### About page

People buy from people. Your About page is where a cautious buyer checks that you are real and established. Include who runs the business, how long you have been operating, where you are based, and a real photo. For B2B businesses, add certifications, capacity, or the industries you supply, if you can state them truthfully.

### Contact page

Put every way to reach you in one place, and make each one tappable on a phone: a call link, a [WhatsApp click-to-chat button](/insights/add-whatsapp-button-to-website), an email link, your full address and an embedded map. Add your working hours. Keep the form short: name, phone, and what they need is usually enough. Every extra field loses some enquiries.

### Privacy Policy

If your website has an enquiry form, a WhatsApp link with tracking, or analytics, you are collecting personal data. A Privacy Policy explains what you collect and why. In India, the Digital Personal Data Protection rules make this more important for every business that collects enquiries online. ## Pages that depend on your business

Add these when they answer a question your customers genuinely ask.

- **Work, Projects or Portfolio.** Essential for anyone who sells visible results: builders, interior designers, fabricators, photographers, agencies. Real photos of real work do more than any amount of text.
- **Pricing.** If your prices are fairly fixed, showing them filters out poor-fit enquiries and builds trust. If every job is custom, a "starting from" range or a page explaining what affects price still helps.
- **FAQ.** Useful when you answer the same five questions on every call. Better still, put service-specific FAQs on each service page.
- **Location pages.** If you serve several cities or areas, a page for each one can help you show up in local searches. Each page must say something genuinely specific about that area, such as projects done there, travel times or local contact details. Copying one page and swapping the city name is something Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) warn against.
- **Industries you serve.** For B2B businesses that supply several sectors, a page per industry speaks each buyer's language.
- **Blog or guides.** Worth it only if you will keep writing. A blog with two posts from 2022 makes the business look inactive. Guides that answer real customer questions can bring steady search traffic over time.
- **Careers.** Useful if you hire often. It also quietly signals that the business is growing.
- **Terms and Conditions, Refund or Shipping Policy.** Required in practice for online stores, because payment gateways in India usually ask to see these pages before approving your account.

## Pages you can usually skip

- **A separate "Testimonials" page.** Few people click it. Put reviews and quotes on the home and service pages, where decisions are made.
- **"Our Mission and Vision."** Fold one or two honest sentences into the About page instead.
- **A news page with no news.** Leave it out until you have something to post.
- **Thin "coming soon" pages.** An empty page is worse than no page.

## How many pages does a small business website need?

| Type of business | Typical page count | What drives it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single-service local business (salon, tutor, plumber) | 5 to 8 | Core pages plus a gallery or pricing page |
| Multi-service business (clinic, CA firm, interior designer) | 8 to 20 | One page per main service |
| Manufacturer or trader | 15 to 50 or more | Product categories, capabilities, industries served |
| Online store | Grows with the catalogue | Category and product pages, plus policy pages |

Do not chase a number. A 6-page site where every page answers a real question beats a 30-page site padded with filler.

## Things every page should have

Whatever the page, a few elements should be there every time:

1. **A clear headline** that says what the page is about, not "Welcome".
2. **A visible way to contact you**, near the top on mobile, and again at the end.
3. **A unique page title and description**, so Google shows something sensible in search results. Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) explains how these appear.
4. **Links to related pages**, so visitors and Google can move from a service to your work and then to your contact page.
5. **Fast loading on a phone.** Most of your visitors are on mobile, and Google mainly looks at the mobile version of your site. If yours is sluggish, see [why a website is slow and how to fix it](/insights/why-is-my-website-slow-how-to-check-and-fix).

## A quick way to plan your pages

Write down the ten questions customers ask you most on the phone or on WhatsApp. Then write down every service or product line you want more enquiries for. Each main service becomes a page. Each question gets answered on the page it belongs to. Whatever is left over goes into an FAQ. That list is your sitemap, and it is usually better than any template.

## FAQ

**Can a small business get by with a one-page website?** For a very small business with one service and one location, a single page can work as a start. But it limits how many searches you can appear for, so most businesses outgrow it quickly once they want enquiries from Google.

**Does having more pages help SEO?** Only if each page is useful and about something different. Pages that target a real service or question can help you show up for more searches. Many near-identical pages do not help, and can hurt.

**Do I need a blog?** Not to start. A blog helps if you can publish genuinely useful posts regularly. If you cannot, spend that effort on better service pages first.

**Should prices be on my website?** If your pricing is reasonably standard, yes. It saves time on both sides. If every job is quoted, explain what affects the price and give a starting point if you can.

**What is the most important page after the homepage?** Usually your service pages. They are where people decide whether you do exactly what they need, and they are the pages most likely to rank for specific searches.
